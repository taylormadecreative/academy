Where the program lives today

A year-long program deserves one home, not five tabs.

The Lab is ambitious: two tracks, thirteen contact points, ten teams each shipping a working prototype for a real business, all reported back to the Interledger Foundation. Right now that runs across a shared drive, a survey form, a calendar, and email threads. Students lose the thread between Wednesdays. Deliverables scatter. And when the funder asks what happened, the evidence has to be reassembled by hand.

Registration → SurveyMonkey

A form that ends at a spreadsheet, disconnected from everything that comes after.

Materials → shared drive

Folders students forget exist by October, with no record of who opened what.

Evidence → rebuilt by hand

Attendance, completion, and outcomes stitched together at reporting time from memory.

The platform

One place, already built, already running.

Not a mockup and not a six-month build. Every screen below is live right now on Taylormade Academy. You can sign in and click through it before you finish reading this page.

01 · Team spaces

Ten teams, ten private rooms.

Each team gets its own space: a live chat, its roster, and a locker with every checkpoint the year asks for. Work lands where it belongs and nothing scatters.

The teams collaborate between Wednesdays instead of going quiet.
02 · Coordinator view

The grant-evidence machine.

Roster, attendance marked in one click, every team’s deliverables, and a one-tap CSV export. The competency and completion data your funder report needs is produced as the program runs, not reconstructed after it.

When Interledger asks what happened, the answer is already a table.
03 · The hub, all year

Every session, kept.

Announcements reach the whole cohort. Every workshop recording and playbook lands here within 48 hours and stays. Miss a Monday, lose nothing. Refer back in March to something from September.

04 · Cohort messaging

The cohort talks to itself.

Direct messages across all ten teams. The students who met at your Data Challenge Finale keep building the network the grant wants them to carry past the program year.

05 · The public showcase

The work, in public.

The coordinator publishes the strongest deliverables to a public gallery: open-source prototypes credited to the students who built them. Your ten published prototypes, one shareable page, ready for Demo Day and the funder.
