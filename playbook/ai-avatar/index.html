Build your own AI avatar in about 15 minutes

This is the exact follow-along from the workshop: the same simplified pipeline behind Da'Sci, the AI avatar host built for the AUC Data Challenge Finale, wearing patches for all 24 HBCUs in the consortium. You'll design a character, make it talk on video, and show it off. Every tool here is free, and you don't need any AI experience. Just curiosity.

What you'll need

Three things, and you probably have all of them already.

A phone or laptop 10–15 minutes A free HeyGen account (no card) A selfie or an imagination, your pick
Step 1

Pick your path

Every avatar starts with a face. You've got two ways to get one: clone yourself, or invent someone new. There's no wrong answer. Pick whichever sounds more fun and go.

Path A

Clone yourself

The fastest route. Take one clear selfie and HeyGen will turn it into a talking photo avatar of you. Want the full clone, one that moves and sounds like you? Record a short video of yourself instead and use HeyGen's Clone a real person option. The free plan includes one custom digital twin, so this is genuinely doable today.

Make your selfie (or footage) count:

  • Good light. Face a window or lamp. No shadows across your face.
  • Look straight at the camera. Head-on beats an angle here.
  • Plain background. A blank wall keeps the AI focused on you.

Got your selfie? Skip straight to Step 2.

Path B

Design an original character

This is how Da'Sci was made: a character built on purpose, with a name, a look, and a point of view. Not typed out and accepted — styled. You're about to do the same thing, and it starts with pulling references.

Go build your reference board below, then come back and generate.

The part that makes it yours

Go shopping for your avatar first

Here's the move most people skip: don't describe a look, show one. Open some tabs and actually pull pictures of the things you want your character wearing. Then hand those images to the AI along with your prompt. Describing "a cool jacket" gets you the AI's idea of a cool jacket. Handing it the jacket gets you yours.

Da'Sci wasn't styled from a sentence. Every piece was chosen — right down to the patches for all 24 HBCUs in the consortium sitting on her jacket. That level of detail only happens when you bring references.

Open a few tabs and save what you like:

  • Clothes. Fashion Nova, Gucci, Pinterest — wherever you'd actually shop. A varsity jacket, a fit you saw and loved, streetwear, all-black everything.
  • Accessories. Sunglasses, hats, chains, hoops, rings, a watch. This is where a character stops looking generic.
  • Hair. Go look up real styles: locs, knotless braids, a fade with a part, a silk press, finger waves, a curly fro, bantu knots. Save the exact one.
  • Vibe. Lighting, background, color palette. A photo whose mood is what you're after counts as a reference too.

Now hand them over. In ChatGPT, attach your saved images to the message, then paste this underneath:

"Use the images I've attached as visual references. Create an original AI avatar character. Name: [your avatar's name]. Role/vibe: [mentor, mascot, guide, host, etc.]. Style: [photorealistic / illustrated / anime / stylized]. Hair: [match the hairstyle in the reference image]. Outfit: [describe which reference to pull the clothing from]. Accessories: [sunglasses, hat, jewelry, whatever you saved]. Personality shown through expression and pose: [confident, friendly, energetic, calm]. Head and shoulders, facing the camera, clean background."

No reference images handy? It still works — just describe the pieces in plain words. References make it sharper, they aren't a requirement.

The pro move

Ask for skin that isn't perfect

AI defaults to airbrushed. Poreless, symmetrical, plastic — and your eye clocks it as fake instantly, even if you can't say why. The single fastest way to make a character read as a real person is to ask for the imperfections on purpose.

Add a line like this to your prompt:

"Ultra realistic skin. Natural skin texture with visible pores, not airbrushed or smooth. Add freckles across the nose and cheeks, a few small blemishes, and a birthmark. Slight asymmetry in the face. Realistic flyaway hairs. Natural, uneven lighting."

Run it once without that line and once with it, and put them side by side. The difference is not subtle — and once you've seen it, you'll never look at an AI portrait the same way again.

Heads up

Free ChatGPT accounts get roughly 2–3 image generations per day, so make each one count. Pull your references and fill in every blank before you hit send.

If the first result isn't right, change one thing and run it again. That's exactly how Da'Sci came together — not a one-shot, refined until it felt right.

Save your final image. That's your avatar's face. You'll need it in the next step.

Step 2

Bring it to life in HeyGen

Now your avatar learns to talk. HeyGen takes a single image, your selfie or your character, and turns it into a speaking, moving video. Free plan, no credit card.

  1. Sign up free at heygen.com. No card needed.
  2. Create your avatar from your image. Choose Photo to Video on the home screen, or Avatars → Create a virtual character, and upload the face from Step 1. (Cloning yourself from footage? Use Avatars → Clone a real person instead.)
  3. Give it something to say. Type a short script (a few sentences is plenty) or record your own voice.
  4. Pick a voice if you're using text-to-speech. This is a personality choice, not a throwaway. Da'Sci's voice was picked for warmth and big-sister energy, not a generic robot read. Choose one that fits who your avatar is.
  5. Generate, then download your clip once it's ready.

Stuck on what to say? Start here:

"Hi, I'm [your avatar's name]. I was made with AI in about ten minutes, and so can you."
