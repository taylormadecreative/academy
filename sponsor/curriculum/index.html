The two hours

Every minute is accounted for.

10 minutes
Opening demo

The Shift

What is taught

The frame for the whole session: you do not need to learn AI, you need AI to learn your business. No tool lecture, no slides about models.

What they watch

A real automation running my real business live: the daily content system that posts for Taylormade while I am in the room with them.

20 minutes
Block one

Your First AI Employee

What is taught

What AI is actually good at for real work: drafting, summarizing, answering, and repeating. How to hand it a messy input and get professional output.

What they do

Live demo first: a raw voice note becomes a client email, a quote, and a follow-up. Then every attendee does the same thing with the task they brought in.

Checkpoint: everyone has one real output
25 minutes
Block two

The Content Machine

What is taught

The voice-training move: feed AI three things you have already written and get ten drafts back that sound like you, not like a robot.

What they do

Each attendee generates a week of content for their own venture, in their own voice, before the block ends.

Checkpoint: five usable posts each
10 minutes

Break

Networking prompt on screen. The room compares outputs, which is where the energy usually spikes.

25 minutes
Block three

Make It Recurring

What is taught

How one-off prompts become a system that runs weekly: reusable templates and saved instructions, so the work does not reset to zero every time.

What they do

Each attendee sets up one simple automation on the task they came in dreading, then tests it once so they know it actually runs.

Checkpoint: automation saved and tested once
15 minutes
Block four

Full Speed

What is taught

What these systems look like when they compound: the posts, the emails, and the reminders behind my public sessions all run on what the room just built.

What they see

The receipts, shown live. Proof, not promises, so nobody leaves thinking the demo was staged.

15 minutes
Close

Wins and next steps

What happens

Recap around the room: two or three attendees share what they built, out loud, in their own words. That is the moment the session becomes theirs.

What they keep

Their automation, their week of content, and the templates and saved instructions they built, all in their own accounts. Nothing lives on my machine.

Tools and accounts

Built to run on free tools.

The session is designed around the free tiers of the major AI chat tools, on purpose. Nobody should have to buy anything to find out whether this works for them.

Setup happens before minute one

Before the first block starts, every attendee is signed into a free AI account, in the room or on the private link. Nobody burns session time on passwords, and nobody gets left behind at step zero.

Their accounts, not mine

Everything is built inside the attendee's own account. When the session ends, their automation, drafts, and templates go home with them and keep working.

The honest caveat

Free tiers carry usage limits, and the session fits inside them. Running these systems every day after the session may take one paid AI subscription, about $20 a month. I tell every room that upfront, and that is the entire tool budget.
