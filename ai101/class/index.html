    Check it, and keep it safe

    The context window is everything the AI keeps in mind: your whole chat. Every time you send a message, it reads the whole chat again. So a longer chat means more to read: it can get slower, it uses up your free messages faster, and it costs more to run. When the window fills up, it can forget the start. So stay in the same chat while you're on the same subject. It remembers what you told it, and a new chat starts from zero. New subject? Start a new chat. Chat getting too long? Ask it: “Sum up this chat so I can start a new one.” Then paste that summary into a new chat.

    A hallucination is when the AI makes something up and says it like a fact. Names, numbers, dates and links are where it happens most. Try it (use your own town if you like):

    Most AI chatbots come with a search or research tool now. It pulls real information from the web and shows you its sources. Look at where each fact came from. Trust government sites, schools, the news and the company's own website. Not Reddit, forums or people's opinions. Now check one yourself. Open one of the links. Does the page really say that? Then paste this:

    Asking the AI “Are you sure?” helps, but it isn't proof. Proof is a second source you find yourself.

    Keep it safe. Before you paste, swap real names, numbers and addresses for [brackets]. What you type is saved by the company that runs the AI, and depending on your settings it can be used to improve the AI. At work, ask one question: “Which AI tools can I use, and what can I put in them?”

    Never paste these into an AI

    • Passwords, PINs or security codes
    • Social Security, bank or card numbers
    • Other people's private information, like health details, addresses or phone numbers
    • Customer or client information
    • Employee information, like reviews, pay or medical notes
    • Company secrets. At work, follow your company's AI rules.

    Pasted something by mistake? Delete that chat. At work, tell your manager or IT right away.

Six ways to level up

  1. Have it ask you questions first

    Not sure what to tell it? Let it interview you.

  2. Show it an example

    AI copies examples really well, so one good example often works better than a long description.

  3. Make it sound like you

    Paste a couple of things you wrote and it picks up your voice.

  4. Use your own language

    Write to it in Spanish, Vietnamese or any language you're comfortable in, and ask it to answer the same way. Have a person check anything important.

10 things to know about AI

  1. How you ask decides what you get. A clear, detailed ask gets a clear, useful answer.
  2. It can be wrong and sound sure. Check names, numbers, dates and links before you use them.
  3. It's a conversation, not a search. Reply to make the answer better. You don't have to start over.
  4. Give it context and an example. Tell it who it's for and what matters. An example is one of the most reliable ways to steer an answer. A role sets the voice.
  5. Slow it down on hard problems. “Think it through step by step” can help with math, plans and tricky questions. Some tools also have a Thinking mode.
  6. It doesn't know everything recent. Its knowledge stops at a date. For anything new, give it the facts, or ask it to search the web if your tool can.
  7. Keep private things out. No passwords, Social Security or bank numbers, or other people's private information. Swap real names and numbers for [brackets] first.
  8. You're the one in charge. You're responsible for what you send, post or act on. Read every answer first.
  9. Use it for a first draft. Ideas, drafts, summaries and explanations. Then add your own judgment.
  10. It keeps getting better. What's hard for it today may be easy next month. Keep trying new things.

When something goes wrong

The answer is too generic.
Add the Context and an Example. Or say: “Ask me 3 questions first, then try again.”
It made something up.
Ask: “Which parts should I check, and where?” Don't trust a “yes, I'm sure.” Check names, numbers, dates and links in a second source you find yourself.
I hit a limit.
Free plans have limits. It can be how much you send, or extras like photos, images and voice. The tool tells you when it resets. Wait a bit, or switch tools. Your prompts work in all three.
It says it can't help.
Say what you need and why, in plain words. It may say no on purpose to anything harmful. For medical, legal or money questions, it can help you get ready, but don't rely on it as professional advice.
It forgot what I said earlier.
Long chats can lose the start. Ask it: “Sum up this chat so I can start a new one.” Then open a new chat and paste that summary.
I lost my chat.
Your old chats are listed in the menu on the left. On the phone, open the menu first. Chats you start in Incognito or Temporary mode are not saved. Keep your best prompts in a note on your phone so you can reuse them.
