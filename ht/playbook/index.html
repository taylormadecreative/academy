The Playbook. How your office runs its space.

Every office on the Hill has a space in the HT Hub. This is who does what in it, the jobs step by step, and the four rules that never bend. Written for the people who will run it, not for a technical team.

01 · Who does what

Four roles. Most people only need one.

A space belongs to an office, and the office decides what goes in it. Nothing reaches a donor, a student, or a trustee without the space owner reading it first.

Role one

Space owner

The VP or director whose office the space belongs to. Advancement's owner is the VP for Institutional Advancement.

  • Decides what the space is for
  • Reads and approves every word before it goes up
  • Names who else can post
  • Is the person a donor or trustee replies to
Role two

Editor

The staffer who keeps the space current. Usually one per office, sometimes two.

  • Sends announcements, agendas, and materials to be posted
  • Keeps the calendar honest
  • Flags anything that needs the owner's read
Role three

Host

Whoever is running the thing on the day: an event, a live session, a track.

  • Reads the check-in code off the screen
  • Answers the host line during the event
  • Reads questions from the room chat aloud
Role four

Member

Anyone with an HT account: a student, a colleague, an alum, a trustee, an admitted student.

  • Checks in, posts in a channel, watches live
  • Adds an agenda to their own calendar
  • Puts the hub on their phone
02 · The jobs

The ten things you will actually do.

Each one takes a few minutes. Where a job needs something published, send it and it is up the same day.

Editor

Post an announcement

  1. Write it in one or two sentences, the way you would say it out loud. Put the date in the sentence, not just the day.
  2. Say which office it comes from, so it carries the right name in the space.
  3. Send it to be posted. It appears at the top of your space's announcement list.
  4. Old announcements slide down on their own. Ask for one to be pulled when it stops being true.
Watch for: "Thursday at noon" reads as yesterday to someone opening the page next week. Write "Thursday, September 10, at noon."
Editor and host

Put an event on the calendar

  1. Send the name, the dates, the place, and one line on what it is.
  2. Send the agenda as a plain list: time, what happens, which room, who is leading it.
  3. Say which items are open to everyone and which are closed.
  4. Once it is up, guests can add both days to their own calendar in one tap, and print the agenda for a folder.
Rooms: use the names your guests will recognize on a sign, not the internal ones.
Host

Run check-in at the door

  1. Ask for a check-in code before the day. Keep it short and easy to hear across a room, like HILL26.
  2. Put the code on the screen at the front, or on a card at the welcome desk.
  3. Guests open the space on their phone, type the code, and they are marked present.
  4. Anyone who mistypes it can try again. Nobody is turned away over a code.
Have a paper list too. A phone dies, a guest leaves it in the car. The code is the fast path, not the only one.
Host

Hold a live session

  1. Send the date, the time, the title, and who is speaking. It appears on the live calendar and on the space's home.
  2. Open the room fifteen minutes early and check your sound. The room shows a test picture until you go live.
  3. Go live from whatever camera you have: a phone on a stand, the auditorium camera, or a laptop.
  4. Watch the chat beside the picture. Read questions aloud so the room and the people at home hear the same answer.
  5. The recording lands on the replay shelf the same afternoon.
Anyone with the link can watch. No account, no app. Say that when you invite people.
Editor

Post materials

  1. Decide who should see it: everyone in the space, or only the people in the room.
  2. Send the file or the link with a title a stranger would understand.
  3. Restricted items show their name but not their contents, so people know to ask.
  4. Replays post themselves after a live session. You only send the extras: slides, a handout, a form.
Never post a student's record, an award letter, or anything with an ID number, even in a restricted item.
Space owner

Open a channel

  1. Say what the channel is for and who belongs in it: a class year, an office, a student organization, a chapter.
  2. Name a lead who will read it. A channel nobody reads is worse than no channel.
  3. It appears in the Community space and in your own space's list.
  4. Welcome the first ten people by name. That sets the tone for everyone after.
Moderation: Student Affairs reviews posts. Anyone can report one in a tap.
Advancement

Send a donor their page

  1. Pick the moment: the ask before a gift, the thank-you after, or the annual update on an endowed fund.
  2. Hand over the facts your office already holds: the fund, the year, what it paid for, who it reached.
  3. Read the draft. The President's note is hers to approve, in her own voice.
  4. Send the link from your own email or a text. It opens like a web page, on any phone, with no login.
  5. It prints cleanly for a visit folder.
Students appear only through the University's own permission process, first names, never a grade.
Advancement

Start a funded cohort

  1. Match a donor to a track that already has dates: AI Literacy, Entrepreneurship, Financial Literacy, Summer Bridge.
  2. Agree the cohort name with the donor and the University.
  3. The gift goes to the University. The University funds the block of seats. No student pays anything.
  4. The cohort appears on that donor's own page: the name, the sessions, and the students it reached, with permission.
  5. Invite the donor to the showcase at the end. That is the moment the gift becomes a face.
Never route a gift to a vendor. The donor gives to Huston-Tillotson.
Space owner

Add someone to your space

  1. They need an account first. It takes a minute and uses their HT email.
  2. Tell us their name, their email, and what they should be able to do: read, post, or host.
  3. They appear on your team list in the space, so members know who to reach.
  4. Say so when someone leaves the role. Access should end the day it should end.
Faculty and staff

Get student work onto the Showcase

  1. Ask the student, in writing, through the University's own permission process. No exceptions.
  2. Send the first name and last initial, the program, and one sentence on the work.
  3. Send a picture of the work, not a picture of the student, unless the permission covers their photo.
  4. If a donor funded that cohort, the same card appears on the donor's page the day it posts.
Never a grade, a GPA, or a full last name.

04 · The rules

Four rules that never bend.

Everything else is a preference. These four protect students, donors, and the University's name, and they hold on every page in every space.

Rule one

A student appears only with written permission, through the University's own process. First name and last initial. Never a grade, never a GPA, never a record. If the permission is not in hand, the student is not on the page.

Rule two

The space owner reads it before it goes up. Every announcement, every agenda, every donor page. Nothing reaches a donor or a trustee on a staffer's judgment alone.

Rule three

The President makes the ask and says the thank-you. Ada opens the door, tells the week, and introduces the students a gift reached. She never speaks for the University, and every line she says is approved before it is produced.

Rule four

Sample stays labeled sample. Anything not yet real carries the word on the page. A page that looks finished and is not costs more trust than a page that says so.

05 · The rhythm

What a term looks like.

A space stays alive on a few minutes a week, not a project plan.

Every Monday

Ten minutes

Read your space the way a member would. Is the top announcement still true? Is the next thing on the calendar the next thing that is happening? Send anything that needs changing.

Two weeks out

Set the event

Send the agenda, the rooms, the people, and the check-in code. Guests who get the link early add it to their calendar and stop asking you where to be.

The day of

Open early

Code on the screen, host line open, live room up fifteen minutes before. Answer the first message quickly. People test whether anyone is there.

The week after

Close the loop

The replay is already up. Post the thank-you, the photos, and the one thing that happens next. This is the week donors and students decide whether it was worth their time.

End of term

Show the work

Student work goes to the Showcase with permission. Funded cohorts land on their donors' pages. Certificates follow when they ship.

08 · Help

When you are stuck.

Something is wrong on a page

Send the link and one sentence on what is wrong. Corrections go up the same day.

You need something published

Send the text as you want it read. Announcements, agendas, and materials go up the same day. A new track or a donor page comes back as a draft for your read first.

An event is coming

Send the dates as early as you have them, even if the agenda is not finished. The page can go up and fill in.

Someone cannot get in

Check the email they used. Accounts use an HT address. If the code at a door is not working, read it aloud and let people in anyway.
